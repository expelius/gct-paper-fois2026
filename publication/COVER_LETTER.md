**To:** The Editors, *Applied Ontology* / FOIS 2026 Programme Committee
**From:** Juan David Zuluaga-Monroy and Diego Fernando Zuluaga-Monroy

**Subject:** Submission of *"The Tensegrity Knowledge Graph: A Holonic Architecture for Scientific Knowledge Representation"*

---

Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration *"The Tensegrity Knowledge Graph: A Holonic Architecture for Scientific Knowledge Representation,"* a long-form research article that synthesises seven classical philosophical traditions — Fuller's IVM cuboctahedron, Koestler's holons, Aristotle's four causes, Whitehead's process philosophy, Wittgenstein's family resemblance, Porphyry's tree, and Boethius's topical maxims — into a single, formally grounded knowledge-representation architecture for scientific research artifacts. To our knowledge, no prior work simultaneously represents causal type, holonic scale, temporal credence, and logical inference over a unified schema; the paper's primary contribution is the demonstration that these classical traditions can be operationalised together in a way that supports formal proof, empirical evaluation, and domain-independent instantiation.

The work is empirically evaluated on a corpus of 1,660 nodes and 2,120 typed edges drawn from an active doctoral research programme; verification scripts, frozen snapshot, and a synthetic-injection ablation harness ship alongside the submission. Three findings are particularly load-bearing: (i) the Boethian maxim engine generates 335 sound inference proposals with zero violations on the released snapshot, and a synthetic-injection ablation confirms an 11/11 = 100% detection rate when violations are deliberately introduced; (ii) the Wittgenstein dual similarity metric achieves 100% family-layer coverage across all four artifact types in the family-eligible subset; (iii) a single 200-line extractor closes the previously-unoccupied CODE slot in a post-evaluation hardening pass, demonstrating the schema's incremental extensibility empirically. A second-corpus biomedical instantiation backs the §6 domain-independence argument with empirical data, not only structural analogy. A first-pass OWL serialisation aligned with BFO 2.0 ships as `gct.owl` to support interoperability with the OBO Foundry ecosystem.

We believe the paper is well-fitted to *Applied Ontology* / FOIS 2026 for three reasons. First, the schema-design methodology — twelve relational slots derived from a non-arbitrary geometric source rather than from intuition or convention — is exactly the kind of foundational-ontology work the venue is designed for. Second, the formal framework (six definitions, two propositions with proofs) provides the kind of rigorous core that distinguishes long-form ontology work from engineering descriptions of knowledge graphs. Third, the relationship to BFO and DOLCE is articulated as complementary integration rather than competition, with a worked OWL fragment demonstrating the alignment in concrete RDF terms.

The paper is submitted as original work that has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration at any other venue. All authors have approved the submission. We have no conflicts of interest to declare. The complete reproducibility package — including the frozen snapshot SHA, the violation-detection ablation harness, the second-corpus biomedical instantiation, the OWL fragment, and the figure-generation scripts — is available alongside the manuscript.

We thank the editors and reviewers for their consideration and look forward to feedback.
